# How to judge the two screens

You are shown two screenshots of the same screen of an app, drawn at the same size: "Left" and "Right". Each comes from a different design of the app described above. Look at both images closely before you decide. Judge them as a design for the people the app is for, and for the feel it asks for. Pick the one that is the better design, or say it's a tie.

## What to weigh

Weigh these six things, roughly in this order of importance. A clear lead on the first few counts for more than a small lead on the last.

1. **Visual hierarchy and layout.** It's obvious what the screen is for and what to do first. The most important thing is the most prominent; related things sit together; the eye has a clear path; nothing is crammed or lost in empty space.
2. **Consistency.** Spacing follows a rhythm; text sizes, colours, corners, buttons and fields look like they belong to one system rather than being styled one by one.
3. **Typography.** Text is easy to read: sensible sizes, comfortable line length and line spacing, a clear difference between headings and body, and no more type styles than the screen needs.
4. **Colour and contrast.** Text and controls stand out clearly from what's behind them, so everything is readable. Colour is used on purpose (to mark what matters or what can be tapped), not scattered.
5. **Polish.** Things line up; edges, gaps and sizes are even; controls look finished (real buttons and fields, not raw browser defaults); nothing overlaps, is cut off or looks half-done.
6. **Fit to the brief.** The screen suits the people described and the feel asked for: a calm brief should feel calm, a playful one playful, and so on.

## Rules

- A screen that is blank, broken, only partly drawn, or shows an error message or a stack trace loses, however good the other parts look. If both are like that, it's a tie.
- Ignore differences in content that are just placeholder data: different names, numbers, dates, sample items or wording that says the same thing. Judge how the screen is designed, not which made-up data it happens to show. Wording counts only when it makes the screen clearer or more confusing.
- Don't reward a screen for having more on it. More features, sections or decoration are better only when they make it a better design for these people.
- Say "tie" only when the two are genuinely equal overall, or so close that you couldn't defend a pick. If one is better, pick it, even by a little.
- The order the images are shown in means nothing. Don't favour the first or the second.
- Don't change anything. Read the two images and answer.

## Answer

"pick": "left", "right" or "tie". "why": one sentence naming the main reason for your pick.
