# 0010 — Rendering SwiftUI screens

- Date: 2026-10-21
- Status: Accepted
- Context: `motif shot` must show a SwiftUI app's screens the way people see them, in every size, light and dark, right to left and in each design variant, without editing the developer's project and without a Simulator window. SwiftUI only draws on Apple's platforms, so this needs a Mac with Xcode. This spike (plan 2H, Task 6) ran on the build Mac mini (Apple M6, macOS 27.0, Xcode 27.0, Swift 6.4, the iOS 27.0 simulator runtime, the "iPhone 18 Pro" device type) on `cli/test/fixtures/swiftui-min`, grown for it into the Xcode 16 "App" template with two screens in a `NavigationStack`, three `#Preview`s (one named), English and Arabic strings in `Localizable.xcstrings`, and a bundled Noto Naskh Arabic registered by `UIAppFonts`. It can be drawn, by a unit-test target hosted by the app in a simulator Motif creates. The harness is `cli/src/stacks/swiftui/harness/` (three Swift files and the scheme, embedded as text), and its runner is `cli/src/stacks/swiftui/harness.ts`. `cli/test/swiftui-render.test.ts` draws two apps. swiftui-min, from a path with a space and an `&`, with Motif's generated theme dropped in: the root in light and dark, a preview right to left in Arabic, one at the tablet size, and a theme probe in the default and partner variants (and in a variant the theme lacks, which must fail). And `cli/test/fixtures/swiftui-xcode26`, Xcode 26's template with SwiftData (main actor by default, a titled `WindowGroup` with `.modelContainer`, an `@Previewable` preview, and a preview that stops the app). Google fonts, Dynamic Type, the other sizes and the safe area were drawn by hand during the spike and looked at; Tasks 9 and 10 test them end to end. Fix round 1 (after review and CI's first run) changed 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 9 and 10 and added 12–15.

## Decisions

1. **Screens are drawn in the iOS simulator by an XCTest unit-test target hosted by the app (`MotifShots`), in a copy of the project.** The test runs in the app's own process, so `Bundle.main`, its string catalogues, asset catalogues and `UIAppFonts` fonts, its `UIApplication` and its window scene are the app's. For each shot the harness (`MotifShotTests.testShots`, reading the options file) makes the view, wraps it, hosts it and draws it:
   - **Hosting.** A `UIHostingController` is the root of a new `UIWindow` on the app's window scene, framed at the shot's size in points. The window's `traitOverrides` set `userInterfaceStyle`, `layoutDirection`, `horizontalSizeClass` (regular from 600 points wide, compact below), `verticalSizeClass` (regular), `displayScale` and, when asked, `preferredContentSizeCategory`. The view gets `.environment(\.locale, …)` and `.environment(\.layoutDirection, …)`.
   - **Settling.** The main run loop turns in 50 ms steps until the window's layer tree (frames and sublayer counts) is the same twice in a row, at most `settle` seconds (5 by default). Every screen in the spike settled in the minimum: a shot took 0.21–0.35 s. **The loop notices layout only.** A change that moves no layer and adds none (a colour or an opacity animating, text replaced in a label of the same size, an image loaded into a frame already laid out, a `.task` that sets state without changing sizes) passes for settled, and the shot can show the screen before it. Task 8 may wait for the layers' contents too, or for the main queue to be idle; this was not measured.
   - **Drawing.** `window.drawHierarchy(in:afterScreenUpdates: true)` into a `UIGraphicsImageRenderer` at the shot's scale, opaque, `preferredRange = .standard` (8 bits a channel, sRGB, whatever the simulated screen's range), written as PNG.
   - **Not `ImageRenderer`.** Rendered alongside in the spike, it drew the whole `NavigationStack` screen, and the `Toggle` in another, as SwiftUI's yellow placeholder with a red "no" sign: it can't draw views backed by UIKit (`NavigationStack`, `Form`'s rows, `Toggle`, `TextField`). `drawHierarchy` drew all of them, `Form` and its insets, the navigation bar's large title and the switch included.
   - **Strings and direction from the environment.** `\.locale` set to `ar` alone made `Text("Welcome")` look up the Arabic in `Localizable.xcstrings` ("أهلا بك", the title "الرئيسية", the button "افتح الإعدادات"): no relaunch, no `-AppleLanguages`, no `-testLanguage`. With `\.layoutDirection` and the trait set to right to left, `VStack(alignment: .leading)`, `Form` rows, the switch and the large title all mirrored.
   - **Sizes.** The four native sizes are Flutter's and Compose's, phones at 3× (a phone's own scale) and the tablet at 2×: `mobile-360` (360×800) gives 1080×2400, `mobile` (390×844) 1170×2532, `mobile-430` (430×932) 1290×2796, `tablet` (768×1024) 1536×2048. All four were drawn on the one phone simulator and looked at; a window larger than the phone's screen (the tablet) is drawn whole. The tablet gets the regular size class (the navigation bar's iPad layout appeared), but the idiom stays the phone's: anything an app decides by `userInterfaceIdiom` is drawn as on a phone.
   - **The safe area is the shot's, not the simulator's.** A window on the iPhone 18 Pro got a top inset of 62 and a bottom of 4 (the window is shorter than the screen), which would change with the simulator Motif picks. So the host ignores the container's safe area (`safeAreaRegions = []`) and the view gets the shot's as `.safeAreaPadding`. Phones get 47 at the top and 34 at the bottom, as Flutter's and Compose's shots do; the tablet and previews get none. Proven with a probe preview (a blue background ignoring the safe area behind red content): the blue was 141 px at the top and 102 at the bottom at 3×, 47 and 34 points. No status bar is drawn.
   - **Dynamic Type** is the `preferredContentSizeCategory` trait: `UICTContentSizeCategoryAccessibilityL` enlarged the system text and a `Font.custom` text alike, and the body wrapped onto two lines.
   - **Fallback: the window's layers.** When the app has no window scene (a Swift package's tests, 3), or `drawHierarchy` answers false, the window's `layer.render(in:)` is drawn instead, and the result's notes say blurs and materials may be missing. In a package's tests `drawHierarchy` printed "[Snapshotting] Render server returned error for view (…, UIWindow)" and gave a blank picture, while `layer.render` drew the `NavigationStack`, its large title, the `Form` and the switch.
2. **The harness goes in by writing the project file's objects directly, from TypeScript (`addTestTarget` in `harness.ts`): no Ruby gem, no separate project.**
   - **Why not the `xcodeproj` gem.** This Mac has `/usr/bin/ruby` and Homebrew's Ruby 4.0.7, and no `xcodeproj` gem. Every developer would need it installed per user (`gem install --user-install xcodeproj`), with its own Ruby, before their first screenshot, and the gem follows Xcode's new object versions (77, with synchronized folders) some time after Xcode ships them. What Motif needs from it is small and additive.
   - **Why not a separate harness project.** It would have to rebuild the app's target by hand: its build settings and configurations, its packages, resources, asset catalogues, string catalogues, Info.plist, entitlements and build phases. A copy of the app's own project builds the app exactly as Xcode does, and the test target only adds to it.
   - **Where.** The harness's sources, the options, `result.json` and (unless the caller names another) xcodebuild's DerivedData go in the run's work folder (`workDir`), a folder Motif owns outside the copy. The copy gets only the patched project file and the scheme. Not inside the copy: a synchronized folder takes every file under it into the app target, hidden folders included. Proven: a `.hidden/Bad.swift` inside the fixture's synchronized `SwiftUIMin` folder failed the app's build ("Expressions are not allowed at the top level (in target 'SwiftUIMin' …)"), so a project whose synchronized folder is its root would have built the harness, and DerivedData's generated Swift, into the app.
   - **What is written,** into the copy only. The project file gets, appended to its `objects`: a `PBXGroup` named `MotifShots` for the work folder's sources (its absolute path, `sourceTree = "<absolute>"`) with a plain `PBXFileReference` and `PBXBuildFile` per Swift file, so it works whatever the object version; sources, frameworks and resources build phases; a `PBXContainerItemProxy` and `PBXTargetDependency` on the app target; the `.xctest` product reference; one `XCBuildConfiguration` per configuration the project has; and the `PBXNativeTarget` `MotifShots` (`com.apple.product-type.bundle.unit-test`). The target is added to the project's `targets` and the group to its main group. Ids are `4D4F54494600` ("MOTIF") and a counter, 24 hex digits, skipping any the file already has. The scheme `MotifShots` goes in `<project>.xcodeproj/xcshareddata/xcschemes/` (it builds the app for testing and tests `MotifShots`, in the configuration of 14). A project that already has a `MotifShots` target is refused: the harness goes into a fresh copy each run. `motif check`'s detection still finds the app target in the patched file (`swiftui-harness.test.ts`).
   - **The target's settings:** `TEST_HOST = $(BUILT_PRODUCTS_DIR)/<FULL_PRODUCT_NAME>/$(BUNDLE_EXECUTABLE_FOLDER_PATH)/<EXECUTABLE_NAME>`, `BUNDLE_LOADER = $(TEST_HOST)`, `GENERATE_INFOPLIST_FILE = YES`, `PRODUCT_BUNDLE_IDENTIFIER = dev.motif.shots`, `SWIFT_VERSION = 5.0`, `SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION = nonisolated`, `SWIFT_STRICT_CONCURRENCY = minimal`, `CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED = NO`, `ENABLE_USER_SCRIPT_SANDBOXING = NO`, and the app target's own `IPHONEOS_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET`, `MACOSX_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET`, `SDKROOT`, `SUPPORTED_PLATFORMS` and `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY`. These, and `PRODUCT_MODULE_NAME` for `@testable import`, `FULL_PRODUCT_NAME` and `EXECUTABLE_NAME`, come from `xcodebuild -showBuildSettings -json -project … -scheme MotifShots -configuration <14> -destination "generic/platform=iOS Simulator" -derivedDataPath …` on the copy (0.5–1.4 s), the app target's entry of its standard output. They are read through the harness's own scheme because xcodebuild takes `-derivedDataPath` only with a scheme: with `-target`, it left a small `SwiftUIMin-<hash>` folder in `~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DerivedData` for every copy. So the harness is written first with the target's name for the module and product, then written again, from the untouched project file, with xcodebuild's values. Only standard output is read: xcodebuild's warnings ("Using the first of multiple matching destinations") go to standard error. `swiftui-render.test.ts` checks that no such folder is left.
   - **The run** is `xcodebuild test -project <copy>/<App>.xcodeproj -scheme MotifShots -configuration <14> -destination id=<udid> -derivedDataPath … -collect-test-diagnostics never CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO COMPILER_INDEX_STORE_ENABLE=NO ENABLE_TESTABILITY=YES`, with `TEST_RUNNER_MOTIF_SHOT_OPTIONS=<workDir>/options.json` in its environment. `-collect-test-diagnostics never` is needed: after a crash (12), and after a SwiftData runtime warning in a test that passed, Xcode 27's xcodebuild ran `simctl diagnose` and waited for it: "Failure collecting diagnostics from simulator: Timed out after 600.0 seconds". Xcode 27's `xcodebuild -help` lists the flag, and CI's Xcode 16.4 accepts it. xcodebuild hands variables starting `TEST_RUNNER_` to the test process without the prefix. A simulator's processes read and write the Mac's own files, so the PNGs and `result.json` (written whole: `result.json.part`, then moved) go straight to their folders.
   - **Proven on two projects,** both drawn by `swiftui-render.test.ts`. The fixture swiftui-min (Xcode 16's template: object version 77, its folder a synchronized group with an exception set for `Info.plist`, Swift 5, iOS 17). And `swiftui-xcode26`, Xcode 26's template with SwiftData, hand-written since xcodebuild can't create projects: `SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION = MainActor` (on the target; in the spike also on the project, which the harness target overrides), `SWIFT_APPROACHABLE_CONCURRENCY`, `SWIFT_UPCOMING_FEATURE_MEMBER_IMPORT_VISIBILITY`, `STRING_CATALOG_GENERATE_SYMBOLS`, an `@Observable` model held in the App's `@State` and passed with `.environment(model)`, a SwiftData `@Query`, and an `@Previewable @State` preview. Its deployment target is iOS 18, not the template's 26, so CI's floor builds it; Xcode 16.4 ignores the settings it doesn't know, so its main actor by default is proven on Xcode 26 and later only. Member import visibility is strict: the fixture's `ObservableObject` needed `import Combine` (without it: "Initializer 'init(wrappedValue:)' is not available due to missing import of defining module 'Combine'"). Both built and drew without warnings from the harness's files.
   - **Not tried:** projects older than object version 70 (Xcode 15 and before, groups instead of synchronized folders; the patch uses only objects they have too), workspaces and CocoaPods, Swift package dependencies (resolved again in the copy), several app targets, and app extensions.
3. **A Swift package app: proven in pieces, not yet drawn by `harness.ts`** (which refuses one, for Task 8). On a package with an iOS 17 executable target holding the `@main` App:
   - the copy's `Package.swift` gets one line appended, `package.targets.append(.testTarget(name: "MotifShots", dependencies: [.target(name: "<app>")], path: "MotifShots"))`, which SwiftPM accepts after the `Package(…)` call; the developer's manifest is never parsed or rewritten;
   - the folder must not be hidden: with `path: ".motif-shots"` SwiftPM silently left the target out (`swift package describe` didn't list it, and xcodebuild said "Scheme PkgApp is not currently configured for the test action");
   - `xcodebuild test -scheme <package's scheme> -destination id=…` ran it, and `@testable import` of the executable target worked;
   - the tests run in a bare `xctest` process with no app and no window scene (`connectedScenes.count` was 0), so the screen is drawn from its layers (1). Its own fonts aren't registered by any Info.plist: Task 8 registers the target's `Bundle.module` fonts with `CTFontManagerRegisterFontsForURL`, as for Google families. The harness already looks for previews in the test bundle's own image, where a package's code is linked.
4. **Screens: `#Preview`s are found at run time; the root is the App's `WindowGroup`, found with `Mirror`.**
   - **Previews.** `#Preview` expands to a struct conforming to `DeveloperToolsSupport.PreviewRegistry` (`static var fileID`, `line`, `column`, `@MainActor static func makePreview() throws -> Preview`), whose name no source can spell (`$s10SwiftUIMin…PreviewRegistryfMu_`). Swift records every conformance in the `__TEXT,__swift5_proto` section of the image that declares it. `MotifPreviews.all()` reads that section (`getsectiondata`) in every loaded image inside the app's bundle (the executable and, in Debug, Xcode 16's `<App>.debug.dylib`, where the code is) or the test bundle, keeps the conformances whose protocol is named `PreviewRegistry`, calls each non-generic type's metadata accessor, and casts it to `any PreviewRegistry.Type`. A preview's id is `<fileID>:<line>` (`SwiftUIMin/SettingsScreen.swift:20`, the line of `#Preview`); its name is the `Preview`'s `displayName` ("Settings"). `fileID`'s first part is the module (`PRODUCT_MODULE_NAME`), which Motif's discovery in the source (Task 7) doesn't know before xcodebuild says, and its second part is the file's name alone, without its folders (`HomeScreen.swift`, though the file is in `Screens/`). So a shot names a preview by `<file name>:<line>` (`HomeScreen.swift:26`), which Task 7 computes from the source, and the harness matches it against the ids' ends (`MotifPreviews.matching`; the full id works too). A Swift module can't have two files of one name, so file and line are unique. All three of the fixture's were found, and the template copy's fourth.
   - **A preview's view.** `Preview` is opaque. `Mirror` shows its body as a closure, `dataSource.preview.structure.singlePreview.makeBody: @MainActor () -> ViewPreviewBody`, with `ViewPreviewBody` private to SwiftUI. `MotifMirror.call` calls a closure that takes nothing whatever it returns: the result type comes from the closure's function-type metadata (kind `0x302`; the flags, then the result type, a word each), the closure is cast to `@MainActor () -> T` with `T` opened from it (`_openExistential`), and called. The view is then the first value inside the result that is a `View` (an `Optional` is looked through). `@Previewable` worked: its view is the macro's `__P_Previewable_Transform_Wrapper`, which holds the `@State`.
   - **Not `_PreviewHost`.** SwiftUI's `_PreviewHost.makeHost(providerType:)` answered nothing for these types: it reads the older `PreviewProvider`. Whether it draws a `PreviewProvider` wasn't tried (Task 7).
   - **This rests on layouts that aren't API** (the conformance records, which are ABI; `Preview`'s stored properties, which aren't). If a later Xcode moves them, the preview fails with "Motif couldn't find the view in the preview at <id>. Tell Motif's maintainers which Xcode you use." The way left is Motif's own: compile each `#Preview` body into the copy, appended to its own file so it reaches `fileprivate` types, as a function the harness calls.
   - **Preview traits** (`#Preview(traits: .landscapeLeft)`, `.sizeThatFitsLayout`) are ignored: a preview is drawn at the shot's size. A `#Preview` of a UIKit view controller has no `View` inside and fails with the message above.
   - **The root (`/`)** is `<App>().body`: `MotifMirror.windowContent` walks the scene for the first `WindowGroup<…>` and reads only its `content` property, which SwiftUI keeps as a closure (`content: WindowGroupRootContent<…>.lazy: () -> ContentView`), and calls it; content kept as the view itself would be taken too. Until fix round 1 it took the first `View` anywhere inside the `WindowGroup`: for `WindowGroup("My App") { ContentView() }` that was the title, and the shot showed "My App" alone in the middle of a white screen (seen). When the content can't be found, the shot fails with "Motif couldn't find the view inside <App>'s WindowGroup. Draw its screens from previews (--screen) instead.", never another picture. Each shot's `view` in `result.json` is the drawn view's type (`ModifiedContent<ContentView, _EnvironmentKeyWritingModifier<Optional<AppModel>>>`), which the render test checks. Modifiers inside the `WindowGroup` come with it (the template copy's root was `ContentView().environment(model).motifTheme()`).
   - **Scene modifiers outside the `WindowGroup` aren't applied, and that can stop the app.** `.modelContainer(…)` on the `WindowGroup`, an `.environment(…)` or `.environmentObject(…)` there, are lost. What happens then is the view's: SwiftData's template (`@Query` with the container on the `WindowGroup`) drew an empty list ("Items 0") on iOS 27, with SwiftData's runtime warning "Set a .modelContext in view's environment to use Query", but a view that inserts into the context, or reads an `@EnvironmentObject` given only there, stops the app with a fatal error, which 12 turns into that screen's failure. Task 8 should say so in the screen's error, and could find a `ModelContainer` in the scene with `Mirror` and give it to the root. Making the App again runs its `init`, and its `@State` is read outside a view: SwiftUI warned "Accessing State<AppModel>'s value without being installed on a View. This will create a new AppModel instance each time." The app's real launch also runs first, as the test's host.
   - **Routes** (a `NavigationStack` path) weren't tried: the root is drawn as it starts. Tasks 7 and 8.
5. **Variants, tint and the main actor (Task 3's review notes).**
   - **`MotifTheme.debugVariantOverride` is set before each shot's view is made and hosted** (`MotifShotApp.setVariant`, with the guide's id, through `MotifVariant(rawValue:)`), for the default's shots too. It is a plain static, which SwiftUI doesn't observe: set after the view was hosted and settled, the shot still showed the default (the spike's "late" shot), so it only works before the first render, and the harness sets it there. Proven by eye: the probe's surface was white for `default` and `#FFF7ED` for `partner`, light and dark, and the default's shot set to `"default"` explicitly was the default. `swiftui-render.test.ts` checks that the partner probe differs from the default's. **A variant the app's `MotifTheme.swift` doesn't have** (the guide gained one since `motif tokens`) fails that shot with "The app's MotifTheme.swift has no variant nope. Run motif tokens again, then motif shot.", rather than drawing the app's own variant; the screen's other shots are taken. An app without variants given one fails the same way.
   - **Tint.** The generated theme's tint comes from its modifier (`.tint(colors.actionPrimary)`), not from the asset catalogue's `AccentColor`, so a preview that doesn't call `.motifTheme()` would be drawn in the asset's accent (the system blue, in the fixture's first shots). The harness wraps every screen in `.motifTheme()` when the app has the generated theme (`MotifShotApp.themed`, outermost, so an app's own inner `.motifTheme()` still applies, and reads the same override). Proven: the bordered button and the switch of a preview without `.motifTheme()` came out in the guide's indigo, and in its lighter dark-mode indigo in dark mode.
   - **The main actor.** The harness's own target is built in Swift 5 with `SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION = nonisolated` and minimal checking, whatever the app uses; the test class and every type that touches the app's views are `@MainActor`, so they can call an app whose types are main-actor by default (Xcode 26's template). Proven with the template-shaped copy: it built and drew. Xcode 16.4 doesn't know `SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION` and ignores it, as it does in `swiftui-compile.test.ts`; CI's job (10) confirms it.
6. **Fonts.**
   - A font the app bundles and lists in `UIAppFonts` is there in the hosted test: the fixture's Noto Naskh Arabic was drawn by `Font.custom("Noto Naskh Arabic", size: 22)`.
   - **Google families** are registered for the process, before the first shot, with `CTFontManagerRegisterFontsForURL(url, .process, …)`, from the files the options list (Motif's cache, `motifHome()/fonts/google/<Family>/`, as for Flutter and Compose). The spike registered Onest, Inter and Readex Pro (variable TTFs from `google/fonts`): `.motifFont(.body)` drew Onest, `.motifFont(.label)` Inter, and right to left the body's Arabic companion, Readex Pro. Unlike Compose, no override hook in the theme is needed: `Font.custom` looks the family up by name when it draws. A file that can't be registered gives a note.
7. **Line height: 1.2 is right for the system font only** (Task 3's review note). Measured in the simulator: the advance between two lines of `Text("Ag\nAg")` (its height less one line's) equals `UIFont.lineHeight` to the pixel, and `.lineSpacing(10)` added exactly 10 points to it.

   | Font | 12 | 16 | 20 | 28 | 40 | Ratio |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | System (SF Pro) | 14.32 | 19.09 | 23.87 | 33.41 | 47.73 | 1.193 |
   | Inter | 14.52 | 19.36 | 24.20 | 33.88 | 48.40 | 1.210 |
   | Readex Pro | 15.00 | 20.00 | 25.00 | 35.00 | 50.00 | 1.250 |
   | Onest | 15.30 | 20.40 | 25.50 | 35.70 | 51.00 | 1.275 |
   | Noto Naskh Arabic | 20.44 | 27.25 | 34.06 | 47.68 | 68.12 | 1.703 |

   The generated `lineSpacing = size × (lineHeight − 1.2)` is within about 0.1 point for the system font, but too loose by 1.2 points for Onest at 16 (a 1.5 body gets 25.2 points a line instead of 24) and by 8 points for Noto Naskh at 16. The space SwiftUI needs is `lineHeight × size − UIFont(name:size:).lineHeight`, which only the font knows. Task 3's code (not changed here) should compute it where the font is: in `MotifType`, at run time from `UIFont(name:size:)` (`NSFont` on macOS), scaled with the text through `UIFontMetrics` so Dynamic Type keeps the ratio; or at generate time from the font file's `hhea` ascender, descender and line gap over its units per em, for project fonts and cached Google families, with 1.2 kept for the system font.
8. **The simulator: Motif's own, created and deleted by each run, with no window.**
   - **The runtime is the selected Xcode's own iOS SDK's** (fix round 1). `pickSimulator` takes `xcrun simctl list -j runtimes devicetypes` and `xcrun --sdk iphonesimulator --show-sdk-version`, and prefers the available iOS runtime of that version (18.5 matches "18.5" or "18.5.1"), so the SwiftUI drawn is the one the app builds against; else the newest available, and the run's notes say "No iOS 27.0 simulator is installed, the version of Xcode's iOS SDK, so the screenshots were drawn on iOS 26.2. Install iOS 27.0 in Xcode › Settings › Components to draw them as your Xcode builds them." The phone is the newest "iPhone N Pro" the runtime supports (its own `supportedDeviceTypes` when listed), else its newest iPhone. Here: iOS 27.0 and the iPhone 18 Pro. Why: CI's first run took the newest, iOS 26.2 on an iPhone 17 Pro, under Xcode 16.4, so the `Mirror` layouts (4) were proven against iOS 26's SwiftUI, not the iOS 18.5 SDK's. The render test logs the runtime and whether it is the SDK's.
   - `xcrun simctl create motif-<pid>-<time> <device type> <runtime>`, `boot`, `bootstatus -b`; xcodebuild runs on `id=<udid>`. Nothing opens Simulator.app.
   - Afterwards `simctl shutdown` and `simctl delete`, in a `finally` and in Motif's exit hook (`onExit`), which Ctrl+C and a termination signal run. Proven: SIGTERM to the runner while `xcodebuild test` ran ended it with 143, and a moment later no `motif-` simulator and no `xcodebuild test` were left; the same while `simctl bootstatus` waited (fix round 1), which was killed too. A delete that fails is in the run's notes, with the simulator's name and udid and `xcrun simctl delete <udid>`. A killed Motif (SIGKILL, or the spike's own `alarm`, which left one booted simulator and an `xcodebuild` waiting on `simctl diagnose` until cleaned by hand) can't clean up; `motifSimulators` lists the `motif-…` devices a later run can delete.
9. **Timings, and the run's time limit.** On the Mac mini, for swiftui-min:

   | Step | Time |
   |---|---|
   | `xcodebuild -showBuildSettings` | 0.5–1.4 s |
   | `simctl create` | 0.15–0.2 s |
   | `simctl boot` until `bootstatus` returns | 14.6–21 s |
   | `xcodebuild test`, empty DerivedData (build, install, launch, 2–19 shots) | 18.3–25.6 s |
   | `xcodebuild test`, the template-shaped copy, empty DerivedData, 3 shots | 41.7 s |
   | `xcodebuild test`, DerivedData kept, a fresh copy at the same path, 2 shots | 15.7–16.2 s |
   | `xcodebuild test`, DerivedData kept, a copy at a new path, 2 shots | 20.9 s (22 Swift compile steps again) |
   | Each shot inside the test | 0.21–0.35 s (19 shots: 0.23–0.28 s); the first 0.3–0.8 s |
   | `simctl shutdown` and `delete` | 3.3–3.5 s |
   | `swiftui-render.test.ts` (4 shots) in all | 38–44 s |

   - A run of the fixture is about 35 s warm and 45 s from empty DerivedData, most of it booting the simulator and building. Shots are cheap: 80 shots would add about 20 s.
   - **CI's first run** (GitHub's `macos-15`, Xcode 16.4, iOS 26.2 then, swiftui-min's 4 shots from nothing): settings 3.6 s, create 0.3 s, boot 78.8 s, test 150 s, delete 6.3 s, 241 s in all. A hosted runner is four to five times slower than the Mac mini, and booting is a large share.
   - **CI after fix round 1** (run 39530834937, iOS 18.5, the SDK's, on an iPhone 16 Pro): swiftui-min's 7 shots took settings 4.2 s, sdk 3.0 s, list 0.6 s, create 0.5 s, boot 69.3 s, test 190.8 s, delete 10.2 s (281 s); the Xcode 26 fixture's 4 shots, one preview crashing, took settings 5.1 s, boot 73.9 s, test 228.5 s, delete 6.3 s (317 s). The whole `swiftui` job's tests took 739 s.
   - A screen that stops the app costs a relaunch: the Xcode 26 fixture's 4 shots, one preview crashing, took 23.8 s of `xcodebuild` (a build, a launch that crashed, and `test-without-building`), against about 20 s without.
   - **The time limit Task 8 uses is 600 s plus 5 s per screenshot,** Compose's rule: a judgement. A real app's first build is minutes, not seconds, and its packages are fetched in the copy.
   - **For Task 8, to be quicker:** build for testing while the simulator boots (`xcodebuild build-for-testing`, then `test-without-building`), and keep the copy at one path with its DerivedData beside it under `<app>/.motif/` (ignored with `*`), re-synced each run: a copy at a new path rebuilt about 5 s more.
10. **CI and floors.** `ci.yml`'s and `release.yml`'s `swiftui` job runs on `macos-15` with Xcode 16.4 selected (`XCODE: "16.4"`, which fails the job when the image lacks it) and its iOS 18 runtimes; it runs `test/swiftui-*.test.ts`, so `swiftui-render` and `swiftui-harness` are in it, and `release.test.ts` lists `swiftui-render` as a heavy SwiftUI test. Xcode 16.4 is the floor; `macos-latest` isn't used, so a runner image's change of Xcode can't move it. This Mac has only Xcode 27, so Xcode 16.4 and iOS 18 are proven by that job, not here. Its first run (39526831993, Swift 6.1.2) drew swiftui-min but on iOS 26.2 (8), and failed `swiftui-compile.test.ts`'s typecheck with "unknown argument: '-default-isolation'": that flag arrived in Swift 6.2. The main-actor-by-default typecheck now runs only when `swiftc --version` says 6.2 or later, and logs that it was skipped otherwise; the other typechecks and the three Xcode builds run on 16.4. **The floor is proven by the run after fix round 1** (39530834937, every job green): on Xcode 16.4 and the iOS 18.5 simulator (the SDK's), both render cases passed: previews found and named, the root through `Mirror` (the titled `WindowGroup`'s content), the partner variant drawn, the unknown variant refused, the broken preview's crash caught and the other screens drawn in a second launch, `-collect-test-diagnostics never` accepted. So the `Preview` and `WindowGroup` layouts hold on iOS 18.5's SwiftUI as on iOS 27's. The APIs the harness uses: `traitOverrides` and `.safeAreaPadding` (iOS 17), `safeAreaRegions` (iOS 16.4), `PreviewRegistry` (iOS 17), `UIGraphicsImageRendererFormat.preferredRange` (iOS 12). The iOS 17 floor holds; the harness built against the fixture's iOS 17 deployment target.
11. **macOS isn't drawn yet.** A macOS-only app needs another way, which the spike didn't settle. In a command-line process, an `NSHostingView` (`sizingOptions = []`) in a borderless `NSWindow` ordered in off screen, drawn with `cacheDisplay(in:to:)` into a 2× `NSBitmapImageRep` (`bitmapImageRepForCachingDisplay` gave 1×), drew a `Form` with its checkbox, button and text field the first time; the second window, dark and right to left, drew only the AppKit text field, even after waiting 2 s. `layer.render(in:)` drew only the text field, upside down. A multiplatform app is drawn through its iOS destination; Task 8 makes `motif shot` refuse a macOS-only app with that explanation for now.
12. **A screen that stops the app fails alone, with its reason; the run goes on** (fix round 1). A `fatalError` in a screen (an `@EnvironmentObject` nobody provides, a precondition, a crash in the app's code) ends the app, and with it the test. Before, `result.json` was written after the last shot, so the whole run came back with no result and no reason.
   - The harness writes `result.json` (whole, through `result.json.part`) before the first shot, before each shot with that shot as `current`, and after it, and at the end with `done: true`.
   - xcodebuild, after the crash, prints "Restarting after unexpected exit, crash, or test timeout" and runs the tests that are left: none, since all shots are one test. So Motif reads the last `result.json`: its shots are kept, `current` names the screen that stopped the app, and the reason is the `Fatal error: …` line of xcodebuild's output (`crashReason`). Every shot of that screen fails with "The preview BrokenPreview.swift:23 stopped the app: Fatal error: No ObservableObject of type Session found. A View.environmentObject(_:) for Session may be missing as an ancestor of this view." (or "The app's root view stopped the app: …"), which also goes in the result's notes, and the screens left are drawn by `xcodebuild test-without-building` on the same simulator, as many launches as it takes. Proven on the Mac by the Xcode 26 fixture's broken preview, and in CI by the same test.
   - No `result.json` at all is never a quiet `null`: a build that failed gives "The app didn't build with Motif's harness:" and the compiler's errors; a test that ran without its options (skipped) says so; an app that stopped before writing anything says "xcodebuild ran the harness, but it wrote no result:" with xcodebuild's last lines; a result with no `current` says the app stopped before its first screen.
13. **Every command is bounded, and its failure is plain** (fix round 1). Every command runs through `execCommand` (or the `exec` the caller gives, for tests): asynchronous, in its own process group, with the time that remains of the run's `timeoutMs`, killed with its children when that runs out or Motif exits. The run then returns `timedOut` with "The screenshots took longer than 25 s and were stopped while drawing the screens. …" naming the step. Proven on the Mac with a 25 s limit: the run ended at 28.4 s (the limit and the simulator's deletion), the simulator gone. Failures say what failed and what to do: `simctl list` ("Motif couldn't list the iOS simulators … Check that Xcode is selected (xcode-select -p) …", no longer "no iOS simulator runtime"), `simctl create`, `simctl boot` ("couldn't start: <simctl's words> Free some disk space, or reinstall the runtime …"), `bootstatus` ("didn't finish booting … restart the Mac's simulator service"), the build settings ("Check that the <App> scheme builds for the iOS simulator in Xcode, then run motif shot again."), and the build. The simulator is still deleted after each. `swiftui-harness.test.ts` runs every path with stand-ins for xcodebuild and simctl.
14. **The configuration** (fix round 1). The harness builds and tests in the configuration the app's own shared scheme tests in (a scheme in `xcshareddata/xcschemes` that names the app target), else `Debug`, else the app target's `defaultConfigurationName`, else the first; the scheme `MotifShots`, `-showBuildSettings` and `xcodebuild test` all use it. `ENABLE_TESTABILITY=YES` is passed to xcodebuild, so `@testable import` works whichever it is (a Release-like configuration has it off).
15. **The audit (Task 10): what the harness can give, not explored.** The audit runs in the same process as the drawing, with the hosted window still on screen, so it can read:
    - UIKit's accessibility tree of the `UIHostingController`'s view: SwiftUI exposes each element as an accessibility element with its label, value, traits (button, header, image, link, selected), frame in the window's coordinates, and whether it's hidden; `accessibilityElements` and `accessibilityElementCount` walk it, and XCTest's own `XCUIApplication` isn't needed (a hosted unit test can't use it anyway);
    - frames, from those elements, for tap targets (44 × 44 points is Apple's minimum);
    - colours, from the PNG (the shot's pixels within an element's frame, as Compose's ATF reads them) or from the layers; SwiftUI's text colour isn't exposed on the element;
    - Dynamic Type, through the content-size trait (1), and reduced motion through `UIAccessibility`'s settings, which a test can't change: SwiftUI's `\.accessibilityReduceMotion` can be set in the environment instead, as `\.locale` is.
    None of this was tried. XCTest's `XCUIElement` audit (`performAccessibilityAudit`, Xcode 15) needs a UI test target and a second process, so it isn't this harness's way.

## Consequences

- Motif can draw a SwiftUI app's screens on a Mac with Xcode and an iOS simulator runtime, without touching the developer's project: everything happens in a copy and a simulator Motif deletes. It can't draw them on Windows or Linux, and `motif shot` there must say so (Task 8).
- The app really launches in the simulator as the test's host, before the shots: its launch code (sign-in checks, network calls, analytics) runs once, in the background window. The drawn screens are made again, apart from that launch.
- The harness relies on things that aren't public API: the layout of `Preview` and `WindowGroup` (through `Mirror`), function-type metadata (to call their closures), and the conformance records of `__swift5_proto`. When a layout isn't found, the screen fails with a plain message; when a cast of a closure or a metadata read goes wrong in a way Swift doesn't catch, the app stops, and 12 makes that one screen's failure. The source-based fallback (4) is the way out.
- A screen can't be drawn apart from what the app's scene gives it: scene modifiers are lost (4), and a screen that needs them may be drawn empty, or stop the app (12).
- A run takes half a minute or more even for a small app, most of it booting a simulator and building; shots themselves are a quarter of a second each.
- Pictures are from the simulator's renderer: the same app may differ slightly between Xcode versions (fonts, control styles: Xcode 27's controls already differ from 16.4's). Motif's tests compare sizes and whether light differs from dark, not pixels.
